Mock-ups for doc/telecmd.md. A writable telemetry is changed where it is displayed: no separate world of controls. Every editor follows the same four rules below. Tags: proposed what the doc decides, choice where two ways are shown, later what the rollout puts after phase 1.

  • The value shown is the reported oneThe big number is always what the device last published. What the page asked lives in a chip next to it, and only until the device answers.
  • One look per request state→ 0.42 pending · ✓ 38 ms applied · kept 1.5 not applied · refused / no answer
  • Disarmed by defaultEditors are there but greyed: the page shows what could be changed, and nothing is sent until the user arms it.
  • Writable is visible before armingA small pen ✎ after the name, in views and in the Telemetries panel.

Arm: can this page move things?

A button in the top menu island, next to the data controls. It only appears when the session has at least one writable telemetry: newcomers who only plot never see it.

AA button with a word proposed

Off, it says Control with a lock, muted like the other secondary buttons. On, it turns red and stays red: it is the one state of the page where a stray click has consequences, and it must be seen from across the room.

  • Shortcut A (free today: Space, T, S, H, E, X are taken). Esc does not disarm (it already means "back to live").
  • While a joystick or a key binding is held, it pulses and says Driving.
  • Disarming releases every continuous control at once; it is the quickest "stop sending" there is.
  • The island does not fade out while armed (it does when the mouse rests, otherwise).

Values view: an editor per type

The row keeps its look; the editor is added under the value. For a number, the bar becomes the slider: the filled bar is the reported value, the thumb is what the page asks.

1Every type, armed

  • Number: drag the thumb along the range (snaps to step), or click the value to type it. Without a range there is no bar: clicking the value is the way.
  • Bool: a switch at the right. While pending, it is striped: asked, not yet true.
  • Choices: a segmented control (≤ 5 choices, else a select). The reported choice is filled, the asked one dashed.
  • Text: click the value, type, Enter.
  • Action: a button with the name. Its last answer is under it.
  • Action with an argument: the button opens a small form, with "pick on map" for a point.

2What happens after a write

The chip says where the request is. It disappears 2 s after "applied"; the other endings stay until the next write or a click on them.

  • Not applied: the device reports something else after the resends: it clamped, rounded or ignored it. The chip says what it kept; the thumb goes back to the reported value.
  • Refused: the device answered with an error; its text is shown.
  • No answer: nothing came back in 2 s (wrong port, device down).

3Confirm, and actions that run

Controls: the values view for driving

Option controls of the values view: tiles, bigger targets (touch screens, gloves), a joystick for a writable xy with rate / hold. A device can ship it: DASH|Robot/Drive:values:drive,motors,mode,speed_limit|controls=1.

1Tiles and a joystick

  • The knob is what the page sends; the small green ring is what the device reports (its echo of drive): the gap between them is the lag.
  • Held: the pad turns red with the arm color, the rate is written under it. Released: the knob springs back, the page sends the rest value a few times, then stops.
  • Keys (WASD / arrows) drive it while the pointer is over the view, or always when the view is focused choice.
  • The device stops by itself hold ms after the last write: written under the pad, so the user knows the safety exists.

Chart: requested vs measured

A writable telemetry on a chart has a second, dashed, step line: what the page asked, as real samples of the page. Not drawn when the page never wrote it.

1Setpoint and response

  • The dashed line is in the same color as the curve; its legend entry reads "→ requested". Click it to hide it, like any series.
  • Where the device did not apply a request, the dashed segment ends in a small cross, with the value kept by the device in the tooltip.
  • The cursor tooltip lists both: speed 0.82 · requested 1.00 (12:01:04.210).
  • For a parameter that is not the curve itself (a target speed and the measured speed are two telemetries), the request line is still drawn on the parameter, and the user puts both on the same chart.

2Drag the setpoint later

A handle at the right edge of the request line, on the latest value: drag it up and down to write. Only armed, only for numbers with a range.

robot.speed (m/s) 0.82→ requested 1.00 robot.current.left (A) 0.80

2D view: drag a goal, pick a point

A writable point (write=xy) or pose is a ghost that can be dragged, in the frame of its telemetry. An action that takes a point gets a pick mode.

1Dragging a writable goal

  • The reported goal is drawn as usual (a flag). Armed, hovering it shows a handle; dragging draws a dashed ghost and the coordinates.
  • Released: the ghost stays, dashed, with a pending chip, until the device publishes the new goal; then the flag jumps there and the ghost goes.
  • Not applied: the ghost turns amber and fades after 3 s, the flag stays where the device says.

2Pick mode for an action

  • "Pick on map" in the action form (or the palette) puts a banner on the view and a crosshair cursor; one click sends the call with that point.
  • The call shows on the map as a short-lived marker with its answer.
  • Esc leaves the pick mode.

3D view: the same, on the ground plane

1A ghost on a plane

Dragging in 3D needs a plane: the ghost moves on the plane z = its current z, in the frame of the telemetry (the ground for a goal). For a writable xyz, Shift drags along z. A pose (position + heading) gets a ring to turn it.

Orbiting stays on the background; a drag that starts on a writable ghost moves the ghost instead.

Image view: pick in the picture

1Pixels or the scene

An action or parameter in the pixel frame of a picture (FRAME_TYPE_PIXELS, e.g. camera.track: "follow this") is picked on the picture as u, v: what the device gets is a pixel of its picture, never a point of a modified image.

A writable point of the scene that the image view draws through its camera (a goal) can be dragged on the picture too: the drag is on the ground plane seen by the camera.

Timeline: when things were asked

1Requests as ticks on the lane

  • A writable telemetry's lane shows each request as a small triangle above it, at the time it was sent: filled when applied, hollow amber when not, red when refused.
  • The band that follows starts where the device reported the change, never at the request: the gap is visible.
  • An action has a lane of its own: a tick per call, colored by its answer (they are log samples).

Log view: the conversation

1What the page sent, between what the devices said

A log view can take the page's requests (they are telemetries of the "this page" source). Outgoing lines are indented with an arrow, answers are colored; with every log of the robot around them, they tell what happened in order.

Command palette

1Type what a device would read

  • Opened with : anywhere (not in a field). The same grammar as the protocol: kp:0.5, !reset.
  • Completes names from the declarations, says the type, range and current value; Enter sends, the line stays to see its chip.
  • Up / down recalls previous lines (a history per session).
  • Disarmed: it still opens, completes, and says "arm to send" (A works from it).

Help: a step of the ladder

1"Settings the page can change"

Like the other steps of the Help panel: a colored message, what it does, the code of the sender (terminal, Python, C++, Arduino), and a field to try it. The Python tab shows the client call rather than raw lines, since the device must also read.

Help

9Settings the page can change
ATTR|kp|write|min=0|max=10 kp:0.4

Declare kp writable, between 0 and 10, and keep sending its value. The page sends back kp:0.42; the value is applied when your device sends kp:0.42 again.

TerminalPythonC++Arduino
kp = tp.param("kp", 0.4, min=0, max=10) while True: tp.poll() # applies what the page wrote motor.kp = kp.value
