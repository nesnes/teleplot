Data menu: mock-ups, round 2

What these build on

Menu 2 of the first round (window, memory, clear), with a circular gauge of the memory used next to the data rate in the button, the heaviest telemetries of menu 4, 5 minutes as the default window, and thinning the oldest data as the default when the limit is reached.

  • Default window: 5 minInstead of 15 s today. Twenty times more data by default: the memory limit matters from the first session.
  • Thinning as the defaultThe oldest data keeps the lowest and highest real sample of each moment. Samples are deleted for good, so the guideline "stored data is never reduced" becomes "never reduced unless memory is full, and then said so". Charts should mark where the thinned part begins.
  • What thinning means per kind of dataNumbers: lowest and highest per slice. 2D / 3D: first and last per slice. Text and images: one per slice. To be confirmed when implemented.
  • The memory figureCounted by Teleplot from its own data (samples, texts, images): an estimate, the same in every browser, and known per telemetry.

The gauge in the button

Three ways to put it next to the rate. It fills clockwise with the share of the limit in use; green, then amber from 70 %, red from 90 %.

Suggested: 1 while there is room, turning into 2 from 70 %.

BTwo columns: settings on the left, memory on the right recommended

Wider and shorter. What you set is on the left (window, what happens at the limit); what you watch is on the right: the same gauge as the button, large, with the numbers behind it and the telemetries that fill it.

Good for
Fits without scrolling, and separates "settings" from "state" cleanly. The large gauge explains the small one in the button.
Watch out
About 60 rem wide: it has to shrink to one column when a side panel is open or on a narrow window.

CWho fills the memory, as one bar

The memory bar is the list: each heavy telemetry is a segment of it, the rest of the bar is what is left before the limit. The policy becomes one line of three choices, with the explanation of the selected one under it.

Good for
The shortest of the four, and the share of each telemetry is read at a glance.
Watch out
Small telemetries make segments too thin to see or click; no room for a "clear this one" action. Only the selected policy is explained.

DNear the limit: what the menu says state to add

The same menu when it matters. The gauge in the button is red and shows its percentage; the menu opens on what is happening and what fixes it, and each heavy telemetry says since when its data is thinned.

Good for
Nobody is surprised: thinning is announced where it happens, with the telemetry responsible and a way out.
Watch out
This is a state of A, B or C, not a fifth design. It needs the datastore to remember where each telemetry was thinned.
Effort
Small on top of the chosen design.

Recommendation

B when there is room, falling back to the single column of A when the top menu is squeezed (they are the same sections), with the warning state of D. The segmented bar of C is attractive but loses the per-telemetry "clear" and hides small telemetries.
