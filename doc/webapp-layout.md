# Webapp layout and motion

```
[left dock: one side panel] [workspace: floating top menu + dashboard] [right dock: edit panel]
```

- **Docks** (`.dock`, `style.css`) hold the side panels. Opening or closing a dock slides its width (`--dock-time`, 160 ms) instead of inserting the panel at once, so the dashboard resizes smoothly and nothing jumps. The left dock has a fixed width for all panels (`--side-w`): switching between Telemetries and Sources only fades the content. The panel stays displayed until the dock has closed (`shownPanel` in `main.js`). **Help has its own dock on the right** (`ctx.helpOpen`, `.dock-help`, a little narrower), so it can stay open next to Telemetries or Sources: a message tried from the Help is seen arriving in the Telemetries panel. In edit mode the Help dock sits left of the edit panel.
- While a dock moves, the edit chrome (pills, "+" slots) is refreshed on every frame (`motion()` in `main.js`), so it follows the views.
- **Motion tokens**: `--motion-fast` (120 ms, fades and small moves), `--dock-time`, `--ease-out`. Everything quick on purpose: the aim is to follow what moves, not to wait for it. `prefers-reduced-motion` sets them to 0.
- **Workspace**: scrolls by itself (the docks stay in place, `.panel-container` is one window high). A band of room for the top menu sits at its top, scrolled away at start, see `doc/top-menu.md`.
- **Welcome** (`components/welcome.js`) is what the main area shows until there is a dashboard: three steps (send, watch, arrange), a button to try the sample data, and the state of the connection to the server.
- **Help panel** (`components/panel-help.js`, design studies `doc/help-panel-mockups.html`, `-2`, `-3`): sending data as a ladder, plus shortcuts and the sample data switch (`components/sample-controls.js`).
    - **"I send data with"**: four tiles (Terminal, Python, C++, Arduino; `HELP_SENDERS`), remembered in `localStorage`. Every line of the panel is written for that choice (`helpLine()`), and step 0 is what a program needs once ("Set up").
    - **The ladder** (`HELP_STEPS`): numbered steps from one number to images. One step is unfolded at a time ("A number" at first): opening another folds it, with a fast slide (120 ms). Labels come second, so that the steps after them can use one where it helps (shapes of one scene, logs read together). A step has a sentence of introduction and three or four examples that build on each other (send everything, then only what moves; a cloud, then the pose of its sensor; an image, then its camera): `tests/help.test.js` also sends the examples of each step in order and checks the result. Each example has the line to copy and three buttons: **Edit** (puts the message in the field at the top, to change it), **Send** (gives it to the page right away, as if a device had sent it; what Teleplot made of it is said under the example) and Copy. Every message is checked against the library's parser in `tests/help.test.js` (add new formats there too); the lines of the terminal and Python were also sent to a real UDP socket. The C++ lines are only the ones its sample shows (`clients/cpp/sample.cpp`); the others say so and show the text line.
    - **Colors**: in every line the name, value, time, unit and flags of the message keep one color each (`helpColorize()`, a display helper that follows the rules of the parser), explained by a key above the ladder.
    - **Try field** at the top: one line at rest (its placeholder is its title), so that the ladder keeps the attention. Focused or typed in, it opens: the message is colored as it is typed, Enter or Send gives it to `TP.parseDataText` as if a device had sent it, and `helpDescribe()` says what Teleplot made of it (or what is wrong). Example chips fill it; "Edit" on an example puts its message there. Esc, "close", a click in the ladder, or leaving it empty folds it back; it is empty again the next time.
