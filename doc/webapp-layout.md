# Webapp layout and motion

How the web application ([`packages/webapp`](../packages/webapp/)) is laid out and moves: docks, workspace, welcome screen and Help panel. The top menu has its own page ([`top-menu.md`](top-menu.md)), and so do the Sources panel ([`sources.md`](sources.md)), the Export and import panel ([`files.md`](files.md) "In the webapp") and the edit mode ([`dashboard-editing.md`](dashboard-editing.md)).

```
[left dock: one side panel] [workspace: floating top menu + dashboard] [right dock: edit panel]
```

- **Sheets**: under 640 px of window, the docks rise from the bottom over the dashboard instead (one at a time, a grip sets their height), see [touch.md](touch.md).
- **Docks** (`.dock`, `style.css`) hold the side panels. Opening or closing a dock slides its width (`--dock-time`, 160 ms) instead of inserting the panel at once, so the dashboard resizes smoothly and nothing jumps. The left dock has a fixed width for all panels (`--side-w`): switching between Telemetries and Sources only fades the content. The panel stays displayed until the dock has closed (`shownPanel` in `main.js`). **Help has its own dock on the right** (`ctx.helpOpen`, `.dock-help`, a little narrower), so it can stay open next to Telemetries or Sources: a message tried from the Help is seen arriving in the Telemetries panel. In edit mode the Help dock sits left of the edit panel.
- While a dock moves, the edit chrome (pills, "+" slots) is refreshed on every frame (`motion()` in `main.js`), so it follows the views.
- **Motion tokens**: `--motion-fast` (120 ms, fades and small moves), `--dock-time`, `--ease-out`. Everything quick on purpose: the aim is to follow what moves, not to wait for it. `prefers-reduced-motion` sets them to 0.
- **Workspace**: scrolls by itself (the docks stay in place, `.panel-container` is one window high). A band of room for the top menu sits at its top, scrolled away at start, see [`doc/top-menu.md`](top-menu.md).
- **Welcome** ([`components/welcome.js`](../packages/webapp/components/welcome.js)) is what the main area shows until there is a dashboard. The logo heads the page, as it is (the file, never redrawn; on a light plate in the dark theme), placed so that its center point (the red ring where its blue traces meet, measured at 60.4 % x 55.8 % of the file) is at the center of the page; a line goes down from just under that ring between two columns. **Send**, under the blue part, each source with its icon (code, USB, folder, robot; red when it cannot work, grey when not available here): from your code (UDP port 47269, a "?" next to the languages that opens the Help written for the chosen one, on its first tile: `ctx.helpRequest = {sender, tile}`, taken by the Help panel whether it is open or not, the line to send in the language chosen in the Help, shared through `localStorage` "teleplot.help.sender" and written by `helpLine()`, Copy, and the path of its data instead of a status line: code, UDP 47269, server, WebSocket; what to do only when it is cut), a serial port (the browser's port picker, or why it cannot; its path: board, USB serial; the two lines an Arduino sends with, `Serial.begin` and a `Serial.println` without the library (`helpSerialLine()`), with Copy; it follows the last port: open = the link green, the port's name by the board and a Close button; lost = red with the reason; closed = back to dashed), a file (goes to the Export and import panel), and the robot sample, made to catch the eye ("Try me", an animated border; no motion with reduced motion). Paths: places as icons with their names in tooltips, words on the links, no "this page" node (every path ends here), muted until there is a state: dashed = nothing came yet (the page cannot tell whether a device sends), solid green = data passed (the address of the first sender of the server replaces the name), red with ✕ = the server does not answer. What came is read from the raw `connection.stats`, twice a second. **See**, under the red part: what can be seen, as the Help's gallery: "Waiting for data: what you send picks the view.", then four families of four tiles of `HELP_TILES` (`GALLERY` in welcome.js: tile ids only), each with its picture (`helpPicture`), its name and one line that makes it (its first example that is data: not `DASH|`, `ATTR|`, `TH|` or a picture; the whole line in the tooltip); a click opens the Help on that tile, in the chosen language (`ctx.helpRequest`). They are the Help's drawings, never data, and "Waiting for data" stays written above them. The page is up to 90 rem wide so that the pictures can be read; four tiles in a row, two by two when the column is under 38 rem (a container query). Under 46 rem of its own width (a container query: an open dock narrows it, not only a small window) the columns wrap, one under the other, without the line. The first telemetry brings the Live dashboard, which takes its place.
- **Help panel** ([`components/panel-help.js`](../packages/webapp/components/panel-help.js), right dock, a little wider than the Export panel: `.dock-help-wide`): "What do you want to see?", a gallery of results, plus how Teleplot works, shortcuts and the sample data switch ([`components/sample-controls.js`](../packages/webapp/components/sample-controls.js)).
    - **Tools**: a search field (titles, descriptions, lines and notes of the tiles, and the concepts; nothing found: the field at the bottom tells what a line is), and under it "I send with", a segmented control (Raw text: the lines alone, as a device writes them; Terminal, Python, C++, Arduino; `HELP_SENDERS`), remembered in `localStorage`: the only place where the language is chosen. Every command of the panel is written for that choice (`helpLine()`): Terminal, the message itself through `nc`; Python, C++ and Arduino, the calls of their client library (`clients/python/teleplot.py`, `clients/cpp/Teleplot.h`, `clients/arduino/Teleplot`: one API), after a set-up shown once ("Set up …, once": the include and `tp`). The parts of the message keep their colors in the calls (`helpCodeParts()`), and follow its numbers when they are nudged.
    - **Tiles** (`HELP_TILES`, in the families of `HELP_FAMILIES`): one per thing Teleplot displays (a curve, a gauge, a timeline, a map, a mesh, a lidar scan, a picture through its camera, a picture in the scene, settings, a joystick...), each with a picture (`helpPicture()`, SVG colored by the theme). A tile opens its sheet in place of the gallery. Getting around without scrolling back: the bar at the top of a sheet stays in sight (`.help-crumb`, sticky: "‹ All", the family, the title of the sheet once its heading has scrolled away, ‹ › the previous and next sheet in the order of the gallery, concepts among concepts: `neighbours`), the end of a sheet has "‹ All" and "Next: …" again, the back button of the mouse and Alt+← go back too, and the gallery comes back where it was left (`galleryScroll`), not at its top. The Help is also the reference of the text protocol: it must be exhaustive ([`tests/help.test.js`](../packages/webapp/tests/help.test.js) checks that the fields of the tiles name every property, shape kind, flag, `ATTR|` key, type, `DASH|` option and kind of line the library knows, reading them from the parser and the library). A sheet reads from the top: the title with the picture as a thumbnail; **The format** (`format`: the general form of the messages of this kind, then `fields`: a table of every part, [part, what it is]); **Step by step** (`examples`, in order, numbered, "Send all"); **Other examples** (`more`, shown, never folded); **Good to know** (`notes`, short points, never one long paragraph); for "Your dashboard", **Options of each view**, listed from the views themselves (`helpViewOptions()`: `getOptionsSchema()` of a view of each type, with its values and default). Every message has a title (what it is for) above its card, and may have a small picture of what it gives (`picture` on an example: the shapes of a joystick view, `joy-*`, drawn in a frame that fits them): a sentence, the message large with its parts named under them (`helpParts()`: name, label, time, value, unit, flags; the properties of `3D|` / `2D|` lines grouped with their values, the keys of `ATTR|`, the limits of `TH|`, the fields of `DASH|` and `ACK|`; a name already given by an earlier message of the sheet is not repeated), pointing at a part (or focusing it) says what it accepts (`HELP_PART_TIPS`), then, small and grey, the command of the language with the message in it, and Send / Edit / Copy; what Teleplot made of a sent message comes under its card, and its numbers are nudged a little (`helpVary()`: values, positions, rotations; never names, times, attributes or texts), so that sending again shows a change. Lines a tile does not need are marked optional (`optional: true`), after the ones it needs. Each tile is complete by itself on a fresh page (it never needs what another tile sent; `DASH|` lines come first, so that the next lines show in the view they create), and a line sent again shows a change: numbers are nudged, texts go through their `cycle`. Times in messages are `{now}` (`helpFill()`: the time of the page, refreshed every second), never a fixed date that would squash a chart. After a Send, the dashboard that displays the result comes to the front (`showDashboardOf`: the one of a `DASH|` line, else one with a view of the telemetry, the current one first; not while editing). "Send all" gives what sending one by one gives (tested for every tile: the views must not depend on how fast lines come; this found that an image telemetry with a place of its own was given an image view when its picture came in the same burst). Tiles of Control say when commands are off, with a button to switch them on. The messages of the panel come from a pretend device of the page (`helpDevice()`: a local connection "Help panel", shown in Sources; `helpSend()`), so the commands the page writes back have somewhere to go: it sends a written setting back as its value (with the flag of its type), answers a call run then ok 0.6 s later, and answers a call sent again without running it twice. Without it the Control tiles could only show controls that could not be used. The conversation of a Control tile also shows what the page sends (`from: "page"` examples: `speed_limit:1.5`, `!reset#12`, in their place between the messages of the device): a dashed card marked "sent by the page", its parts named, no Send (the page sends it when the control is used; "Send all" leaves it out), and under it the last line this page really sent to that telemetry (read from the log telemetry `commands.sent`). A test checks each of them is exactly what the library writes for that control (`settingLine`, `callLine`). `tests/help.test.js` sends each tile alone on a fresh page and checks the views it gives. "Set up, once" (folded) when the language needs it. No related tiles. Files a tile uses (`U:` of a mesh, `T:url:` of a texture) come with the webapp, in [`media/`](../packages/webapp/media/) (`pawn.stl`, `floor-plan.png`; the test checks they exist), and are given by an url relative to the page. A long part (a picture in base64) is displayed by its start and its end (`helpShort()`); Copy and Send take the whole message. A message with double quotes (a text, an url) is put between single quotes in a terminal or Python line, and its quotes are escaped in an Arduino line (the test runs the terminal line in a shell). Tiles of the family "In the page" have no message: their keys and a button that opens the right place (Edit mode, Export, the data menu, Sources, CMD...).
    - **Reference** (a tab next to Examples, remembered: [`components/help-reference.js`](../packages/webapp/components/help-reference.js)): what every value does, written for the language picked (Raw text and Terminal: the property or the line; Python, C++, Arduino: the call of the client, or a dash and why there is none). An index of pages by family (telemetries, shapes, describe, commands, views, dashboards, client; `REF_FAMILIES`, `REF_THINGS`), a page per thing: a drawing whose parts are named in words that do not change with the language and point at what they change (a part that is the whole thing, color or opacity, is named in a corner; `REF_DRAWINGS`, `refDrawing()`), then a row per value (`REF_ROWS`: name, call, what it does, default; opened: the values, its line, the pages it is on, and an example with Send / Edit / Copy as in the gallery). Pointing at a name of the drawing lights its row, and the reverse; a click opens it. View pages also list the options of the view, made from `getOptionsSchema()` with a sentence each (`REF_OPTIONS`; tagged "view", the values sent by a device "device"). Shapes have a page "What applies to what" (`refMatrix()`). The search of the panel searches the tab shown, and each tab says how many hits it has. `doc/reference.md` is written from the same entries (`node packages/webapp/tools/reference-md.js`, `refMarkdown()`); [`tests/reference.test.js`](../packages/webapp/tests/reference.test.js) fails when it differs, when a property, flag, key, type or option of the library or a call of the C++ reference has no row, when a call names something its client does not have, or when an example is not understood; the clients' tests run its examples like those of the gallery (`clients/tools/help_lines.js`).
    - **Nothing above the gallery**: no first message to send, no suggestions, progression or "new" badges: they were tried and dropped (the gallery stays the same for everyone).
    - **How Teleplot works** (`HELP_CONCEPTS`): the data flow (device, server, page, commands back), a telemetry, time (the last sample at or before the time shown, never one made up), views and dashboards, space and frames, commands; each a schematic (`helpSchema()`) and a few paragraphs, and related tiles.
    - **Tests**: every line is checked against the library's parser, the lines of a tile are sent in order and give what the tile says, the pictures are drawn, the parts of every message are named and nudged messages are still understood (`tests/help.test.js`). The calls of the clients are the `lib` of each example (C++, [`cpp-client-api.md`](cpp-client-api.md)), with `py` (Python, [`python-client-api.md`](python-client-api.md); otherwise written from the C++ by `helpPython()`) and `ino` (Arduino) when they differ; a message no client has a call for (`|clr`, the parts of a picture, the answers a client gives by itself) says why (`libNote`) and shows the text line. Every message a device sends has calls in each client or a `libNote` (tested). The calls themselves are run by the tests of each client ([`clients/tools/help_lines.js`](../clients/tools/help_lines.js)): a program of the calls of each tile, whose files must give the page what the text lines of the tile give (names, attributes, kinds of data and their latest values, limits, structures).
    - **Colors**: in every line the name, label, value, time, unit and flags of the message keep one color each (the kinds of `helpParts()`, the one display helper that follows the rules of the parser: anatomy, command lines and the try field all use it).
    - **Try field** at the bottom, always at hand: the message is colored as it is typed and its parts are named above it, Enter or Send gives it to `TP.parseDataText` as if a device had sent it, and `helpDescribe()` says what Teleplot made of it (or what is wrong), above the field. "Edit" on a line puts it there; Esc empties it.
