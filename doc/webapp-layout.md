# Webapp layout and motion

```
[left dock: one side panel] [workspace: floating top menu + dashboard] [right dock: edit panel]
```

- **Docks** (`.dock`, `style.css`) hold the side panels. Opening or closing a dock slides its width (`--dock-time`, 160 ms) instead of inserting the panel at once, so the dashboard resizes smoothly and nothing jumps. The left dock has a fixed width for all panels (`--side-w`): switching between Telemetries and Sources only fades the content. The panel stays displayed until the dock has closed (`shownPanel` in `main.js`). **Help has its own dock on the right** (`ctx.helpOpen`, `.dock-help`, a little narrower), so it can stay open next to Telemetries or Sources: a message tried from the Help is seen arriving in the Telemetries panel. In edit mode the Help dock sits left of the edit panel.
- While a dock moves, the edit chrome (pills, "+" slots) is refreshed on every frame (`motion()` in `main.js`), so it follows the views.
- **Motion tokens**: `--motion-fast` (120 ms, fades and small moves), `--dock-time`, `--ease-out`. Everything quick on purpose: the aim is to follow what moves, not to wait for it. `prefers-reduced-motion` sets them to 0.
- **Workspace**: scrolls by itself (the docks stay in place, `.panel-container` is one window high). A band of room for the top menu sits at its top, scrolled away at start, see `doc/top-menu.md`.
- **Welcome** (`components/welcome.js`) is what the main area shows until there is a dashboard: three steps (send, watch, arrange), a button to try the sample data, and the state of the connection to the server.
- **Help panel** (`components/panel-help.js`, right dock, a little wider than the Export panel: `.dock-help-wide`; design studies `doc/help-panel-mockups.html` to `-4`, then `doc/help-gallery-mockups.html` and `doc/help-sheet-mockups.html` for the sheets): "What do you want to see?", a gallery of results, plus how Teleplot works, shortcuts and the sample data switch (`components/sample-controls.js`).
    - **Tools**: a search field (titles, descriptions, lines and notes of the tiles, and the concepts; nothing found: the field at the bottom tells what a line is), and under it "I send with", a segmented control (Terminal, Python, C++, Arduino; `HELP_SENDERS`), remembered in `localStorage`: the only place where the language is chosen. Every command of the panel is written for that choice (`helpLine()`).
    - **Tiles** (`HELP_TILES`, in the families of `HELP_FAMILIES`): one per thing Teleplot displays (a curve, a gauge, a timeline, a map, a mesh, a lidar scan, a picture through its camera, a picture in the scene, settings, a joystick...), each with a picture (`helpPicture()`, SVG colored by the theme). A tile opens its sheet in place of the gallery. Getting around without scrolling back: the bar at the top of a sheet stays in sight (`.help-crumb`, sticky: "‹ All", the family, the title of the sheet once its heading has scrolled away, ‹ › the previous and next sheet in the order of the gallery, concepts among concepts: `neighbours`), the end of a sheet has "‹ All" and "Next: …" again, the back button of the mouse and Alt+← go back too, and the gallery comes back where it was left (`galleryScroll`), not at its top. The Help is also the reference of the text protocol: it must be exhaustive (`tests/help.test.js` checks that the fields of the tiles name every property, shape kind, flag, `ATTR|` key, type, `DASH|` option and kind of line the library knows, reading them from the parser and the library). A sheet reads from the top: the title with the picture as a thumbnail; **The format** (`format`: the general form of the messages of this kind, then `fields`: a table of every part, [part, what it is]); **Step by step** (`examples`, in order, numbered, "Send all"); **Other examples** (`more`, shown, never folded); **Good to know** (`notes`, short points, never one long paragraph); for "Your dashboard", **Options of each view**, listed from the views themselves (`helpViewOptions()`: `getOptionsSchema()` of a view of each type, with its values and default). Every message has a title (what it is for) above its card, and may have a small picture of what it gives (`picture` on an example: the shapes of a joystick view, `joy-*`, drawn in a frame that fits them): a sentence, the message large with its parts named under them (`helpParts()`: name, label, time, value, unit, flags; the properties of `3D|` / `2D|` lines grouped with their values, the keys of `ATTR|`, the limits of `TH|`, the fields of `DASH|` and `ACK|`; a name already given by an earlier message of the sheet is not repeated), pointing at a part (or focusing it) says what it accepts (`HELP_PART_TIPS`), then, small and grey, the command of the language with the message in it, and Send / Edit / Copy; what Teleplot made of a sent message comes under its card, and its numbers are nudged a little (`helpVary()`: values, positions, rotations; never names, times, attributes or texts), so that sending again shows a change. Lines a tile does not need are marked optional (`optional: true`), after the ones it needs. Each tile is complete by itself on a fresh page (it never needs what another tile sent; `DASH|` lines come first, so that the next lines show in the view they create), and a line sent again shows a change: numbers are nudged, texts go through their `cycle`. Times in messages are `{now}` (`helpFill()`: the time of the page, refreshed every second), never a fixed date that would squash a chart. After a Send, the dashboard that displays the result comes to the front (`showDashboardOf`: the one of a `DASH|` line, else one with a view of the telemetry, the current one first; not while editing). "Send all" gives what sending one by one gives (tested for every tile: the views must not depend on how fast lines come; this found that an image telemetry with a place of its own was given an image view when its picture came in the same burst). Tiles of Control say when commands are off, with a button to switch them on. The messages of the panel come from a pretend device of the page (`helpDevice()`: a local connection "Help panel", shown in Sources; `helpSend()`), so the commands the page writes back have somewhere to go: it sends a written setting back as its value (with the flag of its type), answers a call run then ok 0.6 s later, and answers a call sent again without running it twice. Without it the Control tiles could only show controls that could not be used. The conversation of a Control tile also shows what the page sends (`from: "page"` examples: `speed_limit:1.5`, `!reset#12`, in their place between the messages of the device): a dashed card marked "sent by the page", its parts named, no Send (the page sends it when the control is used; "Send all" leaves it out), and under it the last line this page really sent to that telemetry (read from the log telemetry `commands.sent`). A test checks each of them is exactly what the library writes for that control (`settingLine`, `callLine`). `tests/help.test.js` sends each tile alone on a fresh page and checks the views it gives. "Set up, once" (folded) when the language needs it. No related tiles. Nothing above the gallery: the box with a first number to send was removed (it repeated "A curve" and vanished once data came). Files a tile uses (`U:` of a mesh, `T:url:` of a texture) come with the webapp, in `media/` (`pawn.stl`, `floor-plan.png`; the test checks they exist), and are given by an url relative to the page. A long part (a picture in base64) is displayed by its start and its end (`helpShort()`); Copy and Send take the whole message. A message with double quotes (a text, an url) is put between single quotes in a terminal or Python line, and its quotes are escaped in an Arduino line (the test runs the terminal line in a shell). Tiles of the family "In the page" have no message: their keys and a button that opens the right place (Edit mode, Export, the data menu, Sources, CMD...).
    - **Start here**: with nothing received, a card with the first message and its Send button, above the gallery. No suggestions, progression or "new" badges: they were tried and dropped (the gallery stays the same for everyone).
    - **How Teleplot works** (`HELP_CONCEPTS`): the data flow (device, server, page, commands back), a telemetry, time (the last sample at or before the time shown, never one made up), views and dashboards, space and frames, commands; each a schematic (`helpSchema()`) and a few paragraphs, and related tiles.
    - **Tests**: every line is checked against the library's parser, the lines of a tile are sent in order and give what the tile says, the pictures are drawn, the parts of every message are named and nudged messages are still understood (`tests/help.test.js`). The C++ lines are only the ones its sample shows (`clients/cpp/sample.cpp`); the others say so and show the text line.
    - **Colors**: in every line the name, label, value, time, unit and flags of the message keep one color each (the kinds of `helpParts()`, the one display helper that follows the rules of the parser: anatomy, command lines and the try field all use it).
    - **Try field** at the bottom, always at hand: the message is colored as it is typed and its parts are named above it, Enter or Send gives it to `TP.parseDataText` as if a device had sent it, and `helpDescribe()` says what Teleplot made of it (or what is wrong), above the field. "Edit" on a line puts it there; Esc empties it.
