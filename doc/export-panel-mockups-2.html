Export panel, second round

What is kept, what is new

Six ways to put together the parts chosen in the first round, each shown in two states. They share the same parts; they differ in where the parts sit and how much shows at rest.

  • From 3: three questionsWhat, when, format, with the defaults selected: one click still saves everything.
  • From 6: the checklist, one step away"Choose…" lists the telemetries by label with what each weighs.
  • From 8: files stay as cardsWhat was opened (and saved) in the session, with its period, what went wrong, and "Remove its data".
  • From 10: a receipt before openingWhat the file holds and what opening it changes, with the import options, then one button.
  • New: CSVA third format for spreadsheets and scripts. Numbers and text only; one column per telemetry or one line per sample; empty cells are never filled in.
  • New: CSV files can be openedThe receipt asks which column is the time and how the telemetries are named.

AOne column, everything in place suggested

The panel is one scroll: the save card of mock-up 3, the drop zone, then the files of the session. Picking "Choose…" unfolds the checklist inside the card; picking CSV unfolds its two settings and greys what a CSV cannot hold. A dropped file turns the Open card into its receipt, and after "Open" the receipt becomes a file card at the top of the list.

Good
Nothing moves away: each choice unfolds where it was made, and the recap line under it follows. Saved files are listed too, so "Save again" repeats an export with the same settings.
Less good
With the checklist, the CSV settings and a receipt all open, the panel is tall: the file list is pushed below the fold.
CSV
A third format. Its settings appear only when it is chosen; telemetries it cannot hold are greyed in the checklist with the reason.

BTwo tabs: Save, Open

Saving and opening each get the whole panel. The Save tab holds the three questions, the checklist and the files saved; the Open tab holds the drop zone or the receipt, and the files opened, with their count on the tab. The panel is called "Files".

Good
Each tab stays short, even with everything unfolded. The receipt has room for its options (CSV columns, time).
Less good
A dropped file must switch tabs by itself; the history is split in two; one more click to reach the other half.
CSV
Same third format as A on the Save tab; the Open tab shows the CSV receipt of A when the file is a CSV.

CThree sections that fold, like the Help ladder

Save, Open and the files of the session are three folding sections; opening one closes the others, as in the Help panel. The closed headers still say the essential: how much there is to save, how many files were handled.

Good
Always fits the height of the panel. Same behaviour as Help, so nothing new to learn.
Less good
Only one thing visible at a time: after opening a file, its card is in another section than the receipt that announced it. Extra clicks for people who save and open in turn.

DThe format first, with what it is for suggested for the CSV preview

The three formats are tiles that say when to use each, with their extension. The format comes first because it decides the rest: CSV removes the dashboards and most telemetries. Choosing CSV shows its settings and the first lines of the file as a table, empty cells included.

Good
Newcomers learn what the formats are for without a help page. The preview shows exactly what a spreadsheet will get, and makes the "nothing is filled in" rule visible.
Less good
Tiles take the height of three rows for a choice most people never change; the Save button is further down.
CSV
The preview is the idea worth keeping whatever the layout: it could sit in the CSV settings of A.

EA recap line that unfolds

At rest the panel is two buttons, a line of chips saying what Save will do, and the files. "Change" unfolds the three questions; "Choose…" unfolds the checklist one level further. Folded back, the chips keep showing the choices made.

Good
The lightest resting state, and the history gets most of the panel. The chips keep the "say what will happen" rule without showing controls.
Less good
Two levels of unfolding to reach the checklist. Options are less discoverable than in A: "Change" must be noticed.
CSV
One of the three formats behind "Change"; the chip then reads "CSV" and the dashboards chip disappears.

FReceipts both ways

Saving looks like opening: a receipt that lists the data, the period, the format, what is left out and the file, each line with "change" (data opens the checklist, period the three time choices). One layout to learn for both directions, and "left out" is a line of its own instead of a warning.

Good
The most explicit about consequences, in both directions; the same reading order every time.
Less good
The three questions are no longer visible as controls: changing "what" or "when" is a click on a small link. More text than A for the common case.
CSV
A CSV being opened gets its own receipt lines: which column is the time and in which unit, which columns become telemetries, how they are named.

Suggestion

A, with the CSV preview of D inside its CSV settings. A keeps every part where the eye already is, and its height problem is real only while several things are unfolded at once, which is a moment, not the resting state. If the resting state should be lighter, E is A with the questions folded behind the chips.

What these mock-ups assume and the library does not do yet: CSV writing and reading; remembering which samples a file brought, for "Remove its data" and "Show its period"; reading a file once to describe it before storing it.
