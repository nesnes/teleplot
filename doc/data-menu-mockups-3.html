Data menu: how full is the time window

What the bar should tell

The data menu says how long data is kept (the window) and how much memory it takes. It does not say how much of that window is filled: after two minutes of a five-minute window, charts only go two minutes back, and nothing explains it. The mock-ups all show that situation: a window of 5 min, 2 min 10 s of data held.

  • What is measuredThe time between the oldest sample still held and now, against the window. Counted by the library from the timestamps it stores.
  • To decide: which telemetryTelemetries do not all go as far back (some started later, some keep a shorter time of their own, some were thinned). The summary bar can show the one that goes the furthest (suggested: "how far back can I look") or the shortest.
  • Thinned dataThe part of the history that was thinned out can be drawn differently on the same bar: it is there, with less detail.
  • ClockTimestamps may come from the device's own clock: the bar uses the span of the stored data (newest minus oldest), not the computer's time.

1A fill bar under the window

The simplest reading: a bar that fills from the left as data accumulates, until the window is full. Under it, the time covered and the window.

Good for
Read in a glance, same shape as the memory bar under it: two bars, two limits.
Watch out
It says how much, not where: once the window is full it stays full and tells nothing more.

2A timeline ending at "now" recommended

The bar is the window itself, drawn as time: its right edge is now, its left edge the oldest moment the window allows. The filled part is what is held; the hatched part of it is data that was thinned out to save memory.

Good for
It shows the same thing as the charts (newest at the right) and says where the thinned part is, which is new information now that thinning is the default.
Watch out
Slightly more to read. Telemetries do not all hold the same span: the bar has to show the longest one (or the shortest: to decide).
Effort
Low to medium.

3Time and memory as a pair

The two limits side by side, labelled, with their numbers at the right: how much of the time allowed is filled, how much of the memory allowed is used. The memory section loses its own title.

Good for
Makes the relation obvious: time is what you ask for, memory is what it costs, whichever fills first decides.
Watch out
It reorganises the top of the menu that was just built, and narrows both bars to make room for the labels.

4A bar per heavy telemetry

The summary bar, and the same small timeline on each line of the heaviest telemetries: how far back each one goes, where it was thinned, and the ones that keep a shorter time of their own.

Good for
Answers "why is this chart shorter than the others": each telemetry has its own history, and here it shows.
Watch out
The heaviest list becomes denser, and only covers four telemetries.

5A hairline under the button

Outside the menu: a thin line under the pause / rate control fills as the window does, so it is visible without opening anything. The menu keeps design 1 or 2.

Good for
The first minutes of a session, when "how much history do I have yet" is a real question.
Watch out
The button already carries a ring, a turning mark, a rate and a caret: a fourth signal is a lot, and after five minutes the line is full for good.
Effort
Low.

States of the bar (shown with design 2)

What the same bar says in the situations that matter.

Recommendation

2: one bar under the window presets, drawn as a timeline ending at "now", with the thinned part hatched. It costs one line of the menu, reads like the charts, and makes thinning visible where the window is set. 4 is a natural second step (the same timeline per heavy telemetry) if you want to see which telemetry is short. I would not add the hairline of 5: the button already says a lot.
