Sources panel: mock-ups, round 2

What these build on

From the first round: the health summary on top (4), a status card per source (1), and the serial console (9). Serial ports are opened by the browser itself with Web Serial: no server involved. Remote commands (telecmd) are left out. The six mock-ups differ on one question: where does the console live? They all show the same situation: a local server that works, a serial port open in the browser, and a server that cannot be reached.

  • Serial through the browser"Add a source → Serial port" opens the browser's own port picker; the page never sees the list of ports. Ports already granted are offered again on the next visit.
  • What a serial port needsBaud rate, connect / disconnect, what it receives, a box to send text with a choice of line ending.
  • Lines of a serial portAs in V1: a line starting with ">" is telemetry, the others are log lines. The console shows them all, as received.
  • What is sentShown in the console in blue with an arrow, so a reply can be read against its command.
  • Limit to acceptWeb Serial exists in Chrome, Edge and Opera, on desktop. In Firefox and Safari the serial tile explains it instead of failing.
  • Unplugged, or the tab was reloadedThe card stays, marked disconnected, with "Reconnect": the browser remembers the permission, not the open port.

Six mock-ups

Drawn with the webapp's surfaces, colors and type, at the width of the left dock (D and E need more room, on purpose). In the consoles: green is a line read as telemetry, blue a line you sent, red a line that could not be understood.

AConsole inside the serial card simplest

The health block on top, a card per source, and the serial card opens on its own console: what the port sends, what you send, and the settings of that port, all in the card they belong to.

Good for
One place per source: nothing to select, nothing detached. Closest to the three designs you picked, with the least new chrome.
Watch out
The console is as narrow as the dock and pushes the other cards down; with two serial ports open, the panel gets long.
Effort
Lowest of the six.

BConsole drawer at the bottom of the panel

The cards stay short. The console is a drawer fixed at the bottom of the panel, with a chip per serial port to choose which one it shows; the cards scroll above it.

Good for
Keeping an eye on every source while typing in the console; several ports without making the list longer.
Watch out
The console and the port it belongs to are apart (the chip and the outline tie them). The data controls need another place (the health block's menu).
Effort
Low to medium.

CSources and Console as two tabs

The panel has two tabs. Sources is the health block and the cards; Console gives the whole height of the panel to the serial console, with a chip per port.

Good for
Long sessions in the console: the most lines visible without leaving the dock.
Watch out

DList and detail side by side most room

Selecting a card opens its detail next to the list: the dock gets wider for the time you need it. For a serial port the detail is its settings and a console with room for long lines.

Good for
Reading real serial output (lines are rarely 40 characters wide) and giving every kind of source the same place for its details.
Watch out
A dock that changes width moves the dashboard; it takes a large share of a laptop screen while open.
Effort
Medium (a second column in the dock, and its sliding).

The serial card only shows the last line received, as a sign of life. "Open console" brings a terminal under the dashboard, as wide as the workspace, that stays there when the Sources panel is closed.

Good for
Working with the console and the charts together, panels closed: type a command, watch the curves react.
Watch out
A new region in the layout of the app (the dashboard gets shorter), beyond the Sources panel itself. It is close to what a Log view on the dashboard already does for the received lines.

Highest of the six.

FWho sends what, then cards with their own history most informative

The health block shows how the total splits between the sources, and each card carries the last minute of its own rate. The serial card has two small tabs: its console, and its settings.

Good for
Several sources at once and unreliable links: who sends, how much, and when one dropped, without opening anything.
Watch out
The densest of the six. The curves are the measured rates as they were, not smoothed; they need a per-source history in the library.
Effort
Medium.

Add, reconnect, unsupported

Add
Two tiles, since two kinds of source exist for now. Serial asks for the baud rate first, then hands over to the browser's port picker (it cannot be styled or skipped: it is the browser's permission step).
Reconnect
A port that was unplugged, or a page that was reloaded, leaves a grey card with one button. The telemetries and their data stay.
Unsupported
Said once, in plain words, with what does work.

Recommendation

Start with A: it is the three designs you picked with nothing added, and the least work. Take from F the split of the total between sources if you often have several at once. If the dock proves too narrow for real serial output, D is the upgrade that keeps everything in the Sources panel; E is the one to choose only if you want the console to stay open while the panel is closed.
